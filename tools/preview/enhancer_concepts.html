THE WEAPON ENHANCER AND THE CHIP

The third machine: where a finished weapon takes augment chips (D9), has its Core upgraded in place (D22, D76) and is repaired (D28, D77), all on Forge Energy (D75). Three picks: the machine, the screen, the chip. Every model is pixels (D37). The machines are shown turning with a Laser Pistol loaded and a chip going in (WORKING); the screens are drawn at the game's pixel size and inside a 320 by 240 window; the chip is shown turning, as 16 and 32 pixel icons for each of the seven augments, and with its tooltip.

THE MACHINE set 7

ACRADLE

one block; a scanner over the weapon

A single gunmetal block with a gold rim: the weapon lies in a gold cradle on top under a four-post gantry, and a teal scanner bar sweeps along it while the machine works. The chip rack is on the front (three sockets, lit when loaded), the energy gauge is a column of gold segments on the left side, the repair hopper with an ingot showing is on the right, a readout at the back. Fits anywhere the assembler does not. Mass Effect weapon bench.

BSPIRE

one wide, two high; the weapon hung upright

A tower on a powered base. The weapon stands barrel-up in a gold socket, clamped from above, and a gold ring with teal eyes travels up and down it while the machine works. The chips wait in a drum at the top that turns to the next socket; the energy gauge is the tall column of gold up the spine; the base's front window glows with the stored charge. Reads from across a base. Destiny vault, Star Trek transporter.

CPRESS

two wide, one and a half high; pairs with the assembler

The assembler's sibling, the same two-block base and gold seam. Left half: a steel bed with the weapon on it under a press head that comes down to seat the chip, gold sparks flying. Right half: a stepped console with the chip rack on the first step (two loaded, one empty), the energy readout on the back panel, a repair hopper sunk in the top step with an ingot in it, buttons and a lever. The two machines side by side make a workshop. Halo armoury, Cyberpunk ripperdoc chair.

THE SCREEN

ARACK

gauge left, weapon and chips centre, sheet below
at GUI scale 1 in a 320 x 240 window: the whole screen fits

Reads like a workbench. The energy gauge up the left edge, the weapon turning in its box with its Core tier, roll and slot count, the fitted chips listed beside it with rarity and durability pips. Under them one row of slots: WEAPON, the chip rack (one slot per augment slot the weapon has, pips under each), CORE for the upgrade and REPAIR for the ingot. Keys FIT, REMOVE, UPGRADE, REPAIR, then the status line and the bars sheet with the chips' share in violet.

BBOARD

sockets wired to the weapon
at GUI scale 1 in a 320 x 240 window: the whole screen fits

The weapon is the circuit board. It lies large on the rail; the chip sockets sit above its frame, each wired to the gun by a teal trace, with pips above each socket. CORE sits at the heart under the rail and REPAIR under the grip, where the parts they touch are, as the assembler's Rail screen does (D52). The energy gauge is the column on the right. Teaches where the chips live, as the Rail taught the parts.

CLEDGER

the sheet first, the chips as rows
at GUI scale 1 in a 320 x 240 window: the whole screen fits

For the min-maxer. The energy as a band under the title; the six bars large on the left with teal, gold and violet shares and a red mark where a chip costs a stat (Overcharge's heat cap); the chips as rows on the right, each with its rarity, pips and its own REMOVE key; the slots in one row at the foot with FIT, UPGRADE and REPAIR beside them. The only layout that shows a chip's price next to its gain.

THE CHIP set 8

AUGMENT CHIPS

one item, tinted by its augment (D71)
AUGMENT
A Wafer. A flat square chip: a dark base with gold pins along two edges, a gunmetal body, a dark die on top with the augment's colour running through it as a stepped trace, one keyed corner and a gold index mark. Reads as a processor; sits flat in a slot.
B Cartridge. A round stick (a plus in pixels) with two gold bands, a crystal window glowing in the augment's colour along the middle, a gold contact at one end and a dark cap at the other. Reads as a fuse you push into the frame; the window could dim as its durability goes.
C Shard Key. A tapering crystal in the augment's colour set in a gunmetal clasp with gold teeth, its edges glowing. Reads as a gem keyed to the weapon, Tinkers'-adjacent, and tells the augments apart from furthest away.

AS ICONS, ALL SEVEN

THE TOOLTIP

Piercing, Quick-draw, Stabiliser, Overcharge, Coolant, Extended Cell, Knockback, each at 16 pixels for every look, then 32 for the chosen augment. The tooltip: name; the rarity and augment on a violet line (D81); the chip's own changes; its durability (D83); where it fits.

WHAT TO PICK

  1. The machine (A, B or C), or a mix such as "C's press with B's ring". A takes one block, B one block wide and two high, C two wide and one and a half high like the assembler.
  2. The screen layout (A, B or C), and whether the weapon preview should turn.
  3. The chip (A, B or C).
  4. The chips' share on the bars. As drawn, violet after the gold, with a red mark where a chip takes a stat away; or: chips fold into the gold and only the final number moves.
