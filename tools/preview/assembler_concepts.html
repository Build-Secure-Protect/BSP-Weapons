THE WEAPON ASSEMBLER

Where a pattern, a Core and parts become a weapon, and a weapon comes apart again. Two picks: the block, drawn as a 16-voxel block in the pixel style with the pistol being built inside it (WORKING shows the parts flying in), and the screen, drawn at the game's pixel size with the bars sheet (D38), a catalyst slot for the roll and a PLAN tab for trying parts you do not own (D19). Each screen is also shown inside a 320 by 240 window, the smallest the game allows.

THE BLOCK

ABENCH

a jig on a workbench

A heavy gunmetal bench with a gold two-rail jig on top: the weapon under assembly sits in the jig in the open, drawn full size over the block, and a teal hologram of the missing parts hangs where they will go. A screen and keypad on the front, tool drawers on the sides. The most Minecraft of the three: a workstation you walk up to. Clamps and a vice, Halo and Mass Effect armouries.

BGANTRY

an open frame, the weapon hung inside

Four posts and a top ring with gold clamps, open on every side, the weapon floating at the centre and slowly turning; teal strips run up the posts and the floor plate glows under the work. You see the whole weapon from any side as it builds, and a row of these reads as a factory line. Star Wars hangar, Destiny vault.

CPOD

a dome over the work

A machine base with vents and a gold seam, and a teal glass dome over the top half with the weapon inside, lit from below; a lamp on the front turns from teal to gold while it works. The most sci-fi silhouette and the one that hides the mess; the dome's glass is the pixel-shaded tinted glass of the pack. Star Trek replicator, Cyberpunk ripperdoc.

THE SCREEN

AWORKBENCH

slots left, weapon centre, sheet right
at GUI scale 1 in a 320 x 240 window: the whole screen fits with 32 px to spare

Reads like a crafting table. Pattern and Core slots top left, the pattern's part slots under them in the order the pattern lists them (empty slots are drawn as faint outlines named for the kind), the weapon turning in the middle over its output slot, the bars sheet on the right with the roll odds under it, and the catalyst slot beside ASSEMBLE. PLAN swaps the slots for pick lists and greys the output.

BRAIL

slots laid out as the weapon is
at GUI scale 1 in a 320 x 240 window: the whole screen fits

The slots sit where the parts go. A rail runs across the middle with the Core slot at its heart; the Optic slot above the rail, the Barrel slot ahead, the Cell and Grip slots below, exactly the Spine Rail layout, with the weapon drawn between them. The sheet is a band of bars along the foot and the pattern slot is the rail's end cap. Teaches the gun's anatomy every time you build one.

CSHEET FIRST

the numbers on top, slots in a row
at GUI scale 1 in a 320 x 240 window: the whole screen fits

For the min-maxer. The six bars take the top half with the final numbers large, the before-and-after of the last change shown as a ghost bar; the pattern, Core, parts, catalyst and output run in one row underneath; the weapon is a small turning preview in the corner. PLAN is a side drawer of pick lists, so the bars stay in view while you try parts.

WHAT TO PICK

  1. The block (A, B or C), or a mix such as "A's bench with C's dome".
  2. The screen layout (A, B or C), and whether the weapon preview in it should turn.
  3. Taking apart. As drawn, TAKE APART returns every part and the Core to the slots and loses the roll (D20); or: taking apart costs something, for example the catalyst again.
  4. The catalyst slot. As drawn, one slot beside ASSEMBLE, empty means Normal (D30); or: no slot, the catalyst is placed on the pattern.
