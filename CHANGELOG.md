# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file. The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- The project: a Forge 1.20.1 mod (`bsp_weapons`) that requires BSP-Core 1.17.1 and GeckoLib 4.7.3 and works with BSP-Region Guard, BSP-RankUp, BSP-InventorySync and Mekanism when they are present. Headless GameTests run with `./gradlew runGameTestServer`.
- The part system's data model. A weapon is a pattern (its class, slots and base numbers), a core tier (I to V), parts (each a kind, a type and a material, with its own roll), the weapon's roll (Normal, Rare, Epic, S) and augment chips; its sheet (damage, armour piercing, range, fire rate, charge time, capacity, recharge, spread, zoom, heat capacity, block damage, ricochets, durability) is worked out on the server from those alone. Patterns, materials, bands, part types, augments and catalysts are data files under `data/bsp_weapons/weapons/`, so a datapack can add or change any of them: 105 materials keyed to `forge:ingots/` and `forge:gems/` tags in five bands, 14 part types, 9 augments, 4 catalysts and the laser pistol pattern. Server config `bsp_weapons-server.toml`: the tier and rarity multipliers, augment slots per tier, the piercing and range caps.
- The part items: fourteen parts (Focused, Scatter, Thermal and Pulse Barrels; Energy, Overcharge and EMP Cells and the Magazine; Standard, Light and Heavy Grips; Iron Sight, Scope and Holo Sight) and the Weapon Core (Tier I to V). A part's icon is its own voxel model, tinted by the material it is made of; the tooltip shows its rarity and material and its own changes to the sheet. A BSP-Weapons creative tab. The part data (materials, bands, part types) is sent to every client on joining and on `/reload`, so icons and tooltips match the server's data; players need the same network protocol (1) as the server.
- The Weapon Assembler: a machine two blocks wide, a block and a half high and one deep (a pod with the work under a glass dome on the left, a console on the right, two arms folded above), placed facing the player. Its screen lays the slots out as the weapon is built: the Engram at the rail's end, the Core at its heart, the Optic above, the Barrel ahead, the Grip and Cell below, a roll slot and the finished weapon at the right, with the weapon's sheet as bars and the roll odds. ASSEMBLE uses the Core and one of each part, keeps the Engram, and spends a catalyst if one is in the roll slot to set the roll; TAKE APART spends a catalyst and returns the Core and parts, each with its own roll, while the weapon's roll is lost.
- Engrams (one per weapon class; the Laser Pistol Engram first), the four catalysts (Lapis, Magnatite, Illyrium, Allthemodium: lapis and one ingredient in a vial, with the odds on the tooltip) and the Laser Pistol as an item that holds its parts and shows its sheet; firing comes later. Materials take BSP-Core's colours: Tetrium violet, Magnatite steel blue, Illyrium turquoise.
- Firing. A Laser Pistol shoots a lance of light on right-click: it travels at about two and a half blocks a tick, can be dodged, hurts the first thing it meets, stops at a block and fades at the weapon's Range. Each shot spends a charge, adds heat and a point of durability; at the heat capacity the weapon locks for a moment and loses extra durability; an empty weapon reloads over its Recharge time. Damage is the weapon's own type: armour piercing lets its share of a hit through armour whole, while Protection, Resistance and BSP-Core's totem Resistance see the whole hit. Players are hurt only where BSP-Region Guard allows PvP for both ends of the shot. Kills are the shooter's. Weapons take Unbreaking, Mending and the curses, and no other enchantment. A HUD shows heat and charge while a BSP weapon is held; the held gun's Cell windows and bore flash red when hot and its Core breathes red while locked. Server config `[firing]`: shot heat, cooling time, lock time, overheat durability, wear per shot, lance speed.
- Energy cells. A weapon whose Cell holds energy (Energy, Overcharge or EMP Cell) stores Forge Energy, Capacity shots' worth at 250 FE a shot, and charges in any FE charger at up to a full store per Recharge time; it never refills on its own, and the tooltip shows its FE. The HUD reads NO CHARGE when it is empty. A Magazine cell holds rounds instead. Config: `energyPerShot`, and whether a weapon leaves the assembler full.
- Rounds. The Energy Round, a stackable capsule that every weapon built with a Magazine loads: an empty magazine weapon reloads by itself over its Recharge time when its holder carries rounds, taking up to Capacity from the inventory, and does nothing without them. The tooltip shows the rounds loaded; the HUD reads NO ROUNDS when none are carried.
- Augment chips. One Augment Chip item carries an augment, its own roll and its points: named "<Augment> Chip", its crystal tinted from the augment's data, and in the creative tab for the seven augments that can be fitted (Piercing, Knockback, Quick-draw, Stabiliser, Overcharge, Coolant, Extended Cell); Breacher and Ricochet stay in the data until block cutting and the laser bounce exist. A chip's roll scales its own effect by the part multipliers; the tooltip shows the roll and multiplier, each effect scaled (teal where it helps, red where it costs), the points left and what it fits. A weapon's tooltip lists its chips with their rolls and points. The rules the Weapon Enhancer will apply: fitting spends the chip a point and needs a free socket, a chip the weapon can take and no second of the same augment; taking a chip out returns it with its points; a swap does both in one; an upgrade or a repair wears every fitted chip a point and a chip at zero burns out. No machine yet: the block, its screen and power come next. Augment JSON gains `colour`, `durability` and `available`; the augment data now reaches clients, so the network protocol is 2.
- The Weapon Enhancer: a machine one block wide and two high (the Spire: the weapon stands barrel-up in a gold socket under a clamp, a ring on the spine, a chip drum on top, the energy gauge up the spine), placed facing the player with a companion block above that opens the same screen; breaking either drops the machine. It stores Forge Energy from any cable or charger on any side of the lower block and gives none out. Slots: WEAPON, CHIP IN, CORE and REPAIR (an ingot or gem). FIT puts the chip from CHIP IN into the weapon's first free socket for a set charge; REMOVE takes the chosen socket's chip out into CHIP IN for nothing; a swap exchanges them for the fit charge. Its screen, the Board: the weapon side on over a rail with its sockets above it wired to the frame, each socket a Shard Key with its points, a click choosing one; CHIP IN, REPAIR and CORE under the rail; the charge as a gauge; the weapon's name, Core, roll and chips on the right over its sheet as bars, the parts' share in teal, the Core and roll's in gold, the chips' in violet with a red mark where a chip costs a stat; FIT (SWAP when a full socket is chosen and a chip waits), REMOVE, and a status line that says what the key will cost or why it is grey. UPGRADE takes one higher Weapon Core from the CORE slot, gives the weapon its tier and sockets and leaves the old Core in the slot; REPAIR takes as many ingots or gems of a material fitted on the weapon's parts from the REPAIR slot as it needs, each restoring a quarter of the durability, up to full. Each burns its own charge and wears every fitted chip a point; a chip at zero burns out, leaves its socket and is named in a message. Server config `[enhancer]`: the store's capacity and intake, the fit, upgrade and repair charges, the share one ingot restores.
- Knockback's push. A hit from a weapon carrying a Knockback chip throws the target back about a block along the shot, on top of the ordinary knockback of a hit; the chip's roll scales it. An augment's JSON may name a `push` in blocks, so a datapack can give another chip one. Server config `[firing]`: `pushScale`.
- The Weapon Fabricator's recipes and rules (the machine block, its screen and JEI come next). Parts are made from any weapon material by its `forge:ingots/` or `forge:gems/` tag: a barrel costs three ingots, a cell or grip two, an optic one gem or ingot (the `cost` in each part type's JSON), and a catalyst in the roll slot is spent to set the part's roll. Augment chips are made from one gem and two redstone dust named in each augment's `ingredients`, with the same optional roll. The Blank Engram, a slate crafted on the table from four iron ingots, a redstone dust and a glass pane, is written into a class's Engram with that class's `signature` material (the Laser Pistol: four Tetrium). Weapon Cores (Tier I four iron and two redstone; II four Tetrium; III four Magnatite and a diamond; IV four Allthemodium; V four Illyrium and an Unobtainium), the four catalysts (two lapis and the ingredient) and Energy Rounds (an iron and a redstone make eight) are recipes of the type `bsp_weapons:fabricating` under `data/bsp_weapons/recipes/`, so datapacks and recipe tools can change them; a recipe whose items this server lacks is left out of the machine's catalogue. A job draws its charge when it starts (part 800 FE, chip 1,200, engram 2,000, Core 500 a tier, catalyst 200, rounds 100), spends its inputs then, and puts the product in OUT four seconds later; one job runs at a time and OUT must be empty. Server config `[fabricator]`. The part data sent to clients now carries costs, ingredients and signatures, so the network protocol is 3.
- The Weapon Fabricator block: a machine two blocks wide and one high (the Gantry Printer: a frame over a dark bed with a teal grid, a bridge and head parked at the back, a hopper, spool and gauge on the tower to the left, a console at the right front and a tray behind it), placed facing the player with a console space beside it that opens the same screen; breaking either drops the machine and everything in it, the product under way included. It stores Forge Energy from any cable or charger on any side and gives none out. Pipes and AE2 put ingots, gems, catalysts, dust and Blank Engrams into their slots from any side and take the product out of OUT from below. The chosen row and the job under way are kept by the machine, so a job runs on with the screen closed and survives a server restart. The screen is a plain stand-in for now: the four slots, the progress bar and charge gauge, the chosen row's name with < and > to step through what the machine can make, MAKE, and a status line that says what MAKE will cost or why it is grey.
